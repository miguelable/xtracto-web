Your spending records itself.
And never leaves your phone.

When your bank notifies a card payment or a direct debit, Xtracto reads that alert and turns it into a movement. Nothing to type, no bank to connect, no credentials handed to anyone.

Why you can trust it

Almost every expense app asks you to trust its privacy promise. Xtracto does not ask you to trust it: it declares no internet permission. That is not a policy, it is a technical limitation you can verify yourself on the file that gets installed: 

$ aapt2 dump permissions xtracto.apk

package: app.xtracto
uses-permission: name='app.xtracto.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION'

That is all. That single permission is internal to Android's own libraries and grants access to nothing. There is no INTERNET: even if the app wanted to send your data to a server, the operating system would stop it. No servers, no analytics, no ads, no sign-up, no login.

What it never does

  • ✕Send anything to any server
  • ✕Ask for your banking credentials
  • ✕Read notifications from anything but a fixed list of finance apps
  • ✕Upload your data to cloud backup
  • ✕Show ads or ask you to register

What it does

  • ✓Keep your movements in a local database no other app can open
  • ✓Hide the screen from screenshots and the Recents view
  • ✓Keep the original alert so you can check every amount
  • ✓Let you export everything to CSV
  • ✓Delete everything on uninstall, because there is no copy anywhere else

Check it yourself

You will not have to take this page's word for it, or know how to read code: the claim that matters —that the app cannot connect to the internet— will be checkable against the very file installed on your phone, in a minute, with official Android tools. 

# 1. Find where the app is installed on your phone
adb shell pm path app.xtracto

# 2. Pull that file to your computer
adb pull /data/app/.../base.apk xtracto.apk

# 3. Ask the file itself what permissions it requests
aapt2 dump permissions xtracto.apk

If android.permission.INTERNET showed up in that list, the app could send your data somewhere and everything on this page would be a lie. It does not show up.

How to check it, step by step

Without installing anything

Once the app is published, its Google Play listing will carry the Data safety section, where Google publishes what an app declares about what it collects and shares and checks that declaration against its own analysis.

Your phone's permission screen is no good for this, and it is worth saying so: Android only lists dangerous permissions there, and INTERNET is not one. It never shows up on that screen, for any app, so its absence proves nothing. Here is how to check it properly.

How it works

You grant notification access

By hand, in Android's settings, and you can revoke it whenever you want. It is the only thing the app needs, and without it nothing is recorded at all.

Your bank notifies you, as always

Xtracto reads that alert as it arrives and extracts the amount, merchant, card and date. If two apps announce the same payment, it is recorded once.

You open the app and it is already there

Monthly total, per-card breakdown compared against last month, and the full history with search. Nothing to type.

Notification access, explained

This is the most sensitive permission the app asks for and it deserves an honest explanation. With it, Android lets Xtracto see the notifications that appear on your phone. Xtracto only looks at those from a fixed list of finance applications, which you can review inside the app under Settings. It reads and stores nothing from the rest: your messages, your email and your social networks are discarded untouched.

And because the app cannot reach the internet, what it reads has nowhere to go. It stays on your phone, in a database only it can open.

If your bank is not supported

Xtracto currently understands the alert formats of a specific set of banks. If yours uses another one, the app will tell you: it stores the alert without interpreting it, and you can send us the format without sending us your data. Before anything is shared, the app replaces amounts, dates, merchants and numbers with placeholders and shows you exactly what will go out: 

A direct debit of 41,52 EUR from FUNDACION EJEMPLO
has been charged to your account 1234/**56789

                        ↓

A direct debit of <amount> EUR from <merchant>
has been charged to your account <account>

The template alone is enough to add your bank, and sending is voluntary: the app takes you to the form with everything prepared, or you pick any other route from the system share sheet.

Frequently asked questions

Does Xtracto connect to my bank?

No. It does not ask for your credentials, it does not use Open Banking, and it cannot read your real balance or move money. It only reads the alerts your bank already sends to your phone and turns them into transactions.

Does it need an internet connection?

No, and it cannot have one: the app declares no INTERNET permission. It works entirely offline, and even if it wanted to send your data to a server the operating system would stop it. That is not a promise, it is a technical limitation you can verify yourself on the installed file.

Is it safe to grant notification access?

It is the most sensitive permission the app asks for and it deserves an honest answer. With it, Android lets Xtracto see the notifications that arrive on your phone. Xtracto only looks at those from a fixed list of finance applications that you can review inside the app; it reads and stores nothing from the rest, so your messages, your email and your social networks are discarded untouched. And because the app cannot reach the internet, what it reads has nowhere to go. You can revoke the permission whenever you want from Android settings.

Where is my spending data stored?

On your phone, in a local database no other application can open. It is not uploaded to cloud backup, you can export it to CSV whenever you want, and uninstalling deletes it completely, because there is no copy anywhere else.

Which banks does it recognise?

A specific set of banks, which grows with the formats people send in. If yours is not there, the app will tell you: it stores the alert without interpreting it and you can send us the message template without sending us any of your data, because it replaces amounts, dates, merchants and numbers with placeholders first.

Is there an iPhone version?

No, and for now there cannot be: iOS does not let an application read other apps’ notifications, which is exactly what Xtracto lives on. It is a limitation of the system, not a decision of ours.

When will it be available to download?

It is still in development. What is left is recognising the alert formats of more banks and completing the Google Play release. When it is ready, it will be available to everyone.
